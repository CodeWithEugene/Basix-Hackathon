## 7. All Tracks And Challenges (Full Detail)

The deck groups the tracks into four families. Every team picks exactly one. We list them all because the solo challenges borrow from them and because judges compare across tracks.

### 7.1 Track Family 1: MeTTa Foundation (Level 1)

"MeTTa Foundation tracks that fuse Omega's agent architecture (auditable reasoning, memory, self-correction) with MeTTa's symbolic core." (slide 8)

**MeTTa evaluation criteria:** 30% Technical Innovation, 30% AGI Potential, 20% Implementation Quality, 20% Documentation & Demo.

**Track 01: The Glass Box Agent** (auditable reasoning at the edge, slide 9). "Most AI today is a black box you're asked to trust. This track builds agents that are the opposite: every decision comes with a visible reasoning trail." Choose one:
1. An agent that makes a resource-allocation decision (funding, credit, access) and produces a step-by-step justification a non-technical person could follow and challenge.
2. A "show your work" reasoning agent that can be interrupted mid-decision and asked "why?", and answers by tracing back through its own logic, live.
3. A dispute-resolution agent that takes two conflicting claims and reasons out loud to a resolution, showing every inference.
- Why futuristic: "the difference between AI that gives answers and AI that can be held accountable for them, a real requirement for any AI that touches money, **healthcare**, or governance."
- **Deliverable:** working agent + a sample "reasoning transcript" + 3-minute video demoing a live challenge to the agent's decision.

**Track 02: The Agent That Grows Up** (self-evolving knowledge, slide 10). A small agent that starts with a limited rule set and visibly evolves, updating its own MeTTa rules, with Omega-style persistence across sessions. Choose one:
1. Starts with a wrong or incomplete belief, gets corrected, and shows the literal diff between old and new rule.
2. A community-knowledge agent that starts empty and builds a local knowledge base (for example a campus helper that gets smarter over a week).
3. Flags its own uncertainty, asks a clarifying question, and permanently updates its rule set based on the answer.
- **Deliverable:** working agent + before/after rule diff + 3-minute video showing it learn something live.

### 7.2 Track Family 2: Omega AI Agents (Levels 1, 2, 3) : the family our solo track belongs to

"What's actually cool about Omega/MeTTa (auditable reasoning, self-modifying agents, stateful memory, neural-symbolic logic), each wrapped in a story/product hook instead of a bug-fix exercise." (slide 12)

**Omega evaluation criteria (slide 12):** **30% Innovation & Creativity, 30% Technical Implementation, 20% Sustainability Impact, 20% Documentation & Presentation.**

"All four" Omega design principles (slide 13): every Omega track idea should
1. have a strong one-line pitch a judge or a sponsor would remember;
2. be scoped to "one small agent, one clear behavior", doable by a level 1 to 2 student in 2 days;
3. use MeTTa's actual identity (reasoning, self-modification, memory, symbolic logic) **as the feature, not as homework**.

**Omega Track 01: Two Agents, One Truth** (slide 13). "A pair of agents that have to reconcile disagreeing beliefs about the same fact (one says X, the other says Y) and they have to run a small negotiation/reasoning protocol to resolve it and explain how they got to an answer both can live with." "A fun multiplayer-feeling build, two small agents arguing it out, that taps into the neural-symbolic reasoning identity of Omega without needing the full multi-agent chat infrastructure."

**Omega Track 02: The Agent Without Borders** (reasoning across language and access, slide 14). "Building agents that don't just translate, but reason in a way that holds up across languages, literacy levels, and connectivity constraints." Choose one:
1. A reasoning agent that explains its own decisions in a **local language** (not just translated output, but reasoning steps generated to be understandable to a non-English, non-technical user).
2. A **low-bandwidth/offline-first** agent that makes a decision locally with a small MeTTa rule set, then **reconciles with a fuller Omega-style agent when connectivity returns, and shows how its answer changed and why**.
3. An **accessibility-first** reasoning agent that adapts its explanation style (voice, simplified text, visual) while keeping the same underlying reasoning trail.
- Why futuristic: "It reframes 'AI for accessibility' from a translation problem into a reasoning problem, building trustworthy AI that actually works for the 4 billion people who aren't in Silicon Valley's default use case."
- **Deliverable:** working agent + a short explanation of how it adapted to language/access constraints + 3-minute video.

**Omega Track 03: BASIX.Market Mature Build** (slide 15). "Expects more finished output than Tracks 1 and 2, since it's meant to plug into an actual product." Build a working feature or module for the future **BASIX NFT Marketplace**, such as:
1. A simple ownership/fractional-share tracking prototype.
2. A basic phigital (physical + digital) asset listing flow.
3. A creator-onboarding mini-tool for local artists and entrepreneurs.

### 7.3 Track Family 3: Next Level Devs (advanced, real-world, gaming)

"For teams with prior competition experience... Judged partly on 'would a real business trust this,' not just 'does it work.'" (slide 16)

**The Agent That Can Be Trusted With Money** (slide 17). Choose one: a vendor/quote comparison agent (3 to 4 options, cost/risk/fit reasoning); a micro-lending/fractional-ownership approval agent (BASIX-flavoured) with justification and stated confidence; a pricing/discount agent that explains line by line. Bar raisers: must handle at least one case of incomplete/conflicting input and explain how it handled the gap; must produce a justification a human reviewer could act on to **override** the agent. **Deliverable:** working agent + one-page "audit trail" sample + 3-minute video pitched as if to a business owner.

**The NPC That Won't Break Character** (slide 18). Choose one: a quest-giver NPC that remembers and refuses to repeat quests; a rival/companion with a consistent opinion it can explain; a "world knows about you" agent sourcing which NPC told it what. Memory has to visibly change behaviour in-session. Bonus: the NPC can be "wrong" (acting on a rumour) as a feature. **Deliverable:** working demo + play-session transcript + 3-minute video.

### 7.4 Track Family 4: AI Infrastructure Layer (advanced)

"This is not a classroom exercise." Teams work in their GitHub as a live MVP environment using SingularityNET's Omega agent infrastructure, "a neural-symbolic agent framework on the Hyperon AGI stack built for stateful agents with auditable reasoning and long-term persistence", to design real tools for universities, businesses and community ecosystems (slide 19). Choose one: **Dev/Scale Support** (onboarding, CRM, automation, reporting, customer service, ops, lead qualification for small businesses); **Fractional CTO Agent** (technical planning, roadmaps, vendor evaluation, dev briefs, MVP planning); **RWA / Digital Asset Infrastructure** (managing, documenting or activating real-world assets, cultural IP, student projects, public art, sponsorship assets, tokenised proof-of-value). **Eligibility:** students who completed a prior MeTTa track or BASIX.Market coursework.

## 8. The Omega Solo Track (Our Track)

Full text from deck slides 26 and 27, with our reading.

> "Open to individual hackers building across the two build days with October 2 to polish, record and submit. Every other track lets teams choose Omega, MeTTa, or both. This one doesn't: if you're building solo, you're building on Omega, full stop. The trade-off for working alone is a tighter, sharper scope: **you're not shipping a platform, you're proving one idea works.**"

### 8.1 The Challenge (choose one)

1. **One agent producing an auditable decision**
2. **One agent learning and updating a rule**
3. **Two agents resolving one conflicting claim**
4. **One NPC demonstrating persistent memory**
5. **One BASIX workflow completing a defined business task**

### 8.2 Hard Requirements

1. **Built on Omega, not optional.** "Some visible piece of **Omega's stateful, auditable-reasoning architecture** has to be the actual feature, not decoration."
2. **AI Disclosure, mandatory.** "Every submission includes a short, plain statement of which AI tools or models you used during the build and how (for example 'Claude for code review and debugging', 'GPT-5 for boilerplate scaffolding', 'Omega's own inference for the agent's reasoning loop'). This isn't a penalty: AI-assisted building on a solo build is expected. **Undisclosed use, if discovered, is a disqualifying integrity issue; disclosed use is not.**"
3. **"No solo hacker attempts a complete platform. One feature, proven, is the whole assignment."**

### 8.3 Solo Deliverables (due 11:59 PM IST, 2 October)

- Working GitHub repository shared with BASIX.Market
- Short README: problem, solution, technology, plus the AI Disclosure statement
- One functioning Omega feature
- Sample reasoning transcript, memory record, or audit trail
- 3-minute demonstration video
- Short statement of what you would build next

### 8.4 How The Solo Track Maps Onto The Platform Form

The form has no Solo option (platform JS). We select **Track: Omega** and then the Omega problem that matches our feature. The README and description should also say plainly which **solo challenge number** we answer, so the eligibility check on Saturday morning is trivial for staff.

## 10. Judging Criteria, Rubrics And Judging Plan

### 10.1 The All-Tracks Rubric (deck slides 22 and 27, "Scoring Remains The Same")

| Weight | Criterion | What earns full marks (our reading) |
|---|---|---|
| **30%** | Technical execution | It runs, end to end, from a clean clone, with one command. Real Omega/MeTTa doing real work. Tests. No crashes in the demo. |
| **25%** | Clarity of the 3-minute video / demo | One story, one feature, live. A judge can repeat the pitch after watching once. Captions. Under 3:00. |
| **25%** | Fit to track (Omega / MeTTa / BASIX) | Omega's stateful, auditable reasoning is the feature. The README names the Omega problem and the solo challenge. |
| **20%** | Documentation & build process | README with problem, solution, technology, AI Disclosure, what's next. A build log. Sample transcript or audit trail. Setup steps that work. |

### 10.2 The Omega Track Rubric (deck slide 12)

| Weight | Criterion |
|---|---|
| 30% | Innovation & Creativity |
| 30% | Technical Implementation |
| **20%** | **Sustainability Impact** |
| 20% | Documentation & Presentation |

Judges may use either or both. We design to satisfy both: the overlap is technical quality, documentation and presentation. The Omega rubric adds **innovation** and **sustainability impact**, which reward a real-world problem with a credible path to adoption.

### 10.3 Judging Plan (deck slide 27)

"We do not require international judges to assess everything live. This plan applies to all tracks."

1. **Sat 3 Oct, 10:30 to 14:00 IST (08:00 to 11:30 EAT):** BASIX and technical staff verify eligible submissions.
2. **Sat 3 Oct, 14:00 to 18:00 IST (11:30 to 15:30 EAT):** judges receive repositories, documentation and three-minute videos.
3. **Sat 3 Oct, 19:30 to 21:00 IST (17:00 to 18:30 EAT):** virtual finalist presentations.
4. Winners are announced after presentations.

**Consequence:** most judges will meet our project through the **README and the video first**, asynchronously, in a four-hour window. The README's first screen and the video's first 20 seconds decide whether we make the finalist call.

## 21. What Wins First Place Here

This section turns everything above into rules for our build. The solution itself is in [solution.md](solution.md) and the build spec in [build.md](build.md).

1. **Real Omega, visibly.** Load Omega's own `lib_nal.metta` from a pinned `singnet/Omega` commit, run it on PeTTa (Omega's runtime), package our logic as a real Omega plugin, and show `(stv f c)` truth values in the UI. Several rivals only claim Omega. Two judges commit to the Omega repo.
2. **One feature, proven, live.** The solo brief says it twice. Every minute of build time goes into making one loop flawless.
3. **Memory that changes the answer.** Every BGI winner had memory that visibly mattered. The demo must show the same input producing a different, better decision because of what the agent remembers.
4. **Human in the loop.** Past winners had human approval or override. The person receiving the agent's decision can challenge it, and the agent recomputes.
5. **An empty lane with a Kenyan story.** "The Agent Without Borders" has no public entry. Kenyan context aligns with the KU MOU, Sheila Wanjiru's remit and the 20% Sustainability Impact criterion.
6. **A video that could air on YouTube.** 25% of the score, and half the panel are media and business people. One human story, live product, captions, under 3:00.
7. **Documentation that does the judging for them.** The README's first screen says what it is, which track, which solo challenge, how to run it in one command, and the AI Disclosure. Include a reasoning transcript and an audit trail in `docs/`.
8. **Honesty as a feature.** Synthetic data only, clear disclaimer, credit every dependency, state limitations. This matches the ecosystem's values and Haley Lowy's own commits.
9. **A "what's next" that the hosts can act on.** KU/ESA-KU incubation, a county pilot, a BGI Nexus grant, and the 24 to 25 October BASIX hackathon.
10. **Submit early.** By 21:00 EAT. Be ready for the 17:00 EAT finalist call on Saturday.
